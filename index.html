Arrow Grid — Privacy Policy

Effective <RELEASE DATE>

Arrow Grid ("the game") is made by Atish Agrawal, an independent developer. This policy explains what the game does with your information. In short: the game gives every player an anonymous profile with a random generated name (never your real name) so you can appear on public leaderboards. The game does not collect your name, email or phone number. It uses a few third-party services (Unity Gaming Services for profiles and leaderboards, ads, anonymous analytics and crash reports, Google Play purchases and Google Play Games) that process some technical data as described below. The game requests non-personalised ads.

What the game collects

Arrow Grid creates an anonymous player profile for every player automatically, and supports optional sign-in with Google Play Games Services. The game works fully without signing in. It shows a short ad after you finish a level, offers optional rewarded ads and optional in-app purchases of hints, and uses anonymous analytics and crash reporting.

Google Play Games (optional)

If you sign in to Google Play Games, the game sends your achievements and best times to Google Play Games Services so they can appear on your achievements list. This uses your Play Games profile (player ID, gamer name and avatar), which Google provides to the game. The developer does not receive or store this data on its own servers; Google processes it under the Google Play Games Services terms and the Google Privacy Policy. The Play Games SDK may also send diagnostics and analytics data to Google to keep the service stable.

If you sign in to Google Play Games, the game also keeps a copy of your game progress (levels completed, best moves, stars, hints used, hint balance, streaks, records, weekly cup standing and settings) in Google Play Games Saved Games, so your progress can be restored on another device or after you reinstall the game. Saved Games stores this copy with your Google Account; the developer does not receive it or store it on its own servers. If you don't sign in, nothing is saved this way.

If you sign in to Google Play Games and allow it when Google asks, the game's Friends page shows your Play Games friends (gamer name and levels cleared on the game's leaderboard) so you can compare progress. This list is shown on your device only; the game does not store it or send it anywhere, and the developer never receives it.

Player profiles and leaderboards

The first time you open the game online, it creates an anonymous player profile for you, without asking you to sign up. The profile is a Unity Authentication anonymous account with a random generated name such as "SwiftOtter#12345". You cannot choose or change the name, and the game never uses your real name for it. Your profile is created for every player the same way, whatever their age.

What other players can see. The game has three public leaderboards: levels completed, weekly cup points, and daily puzzle time. Anyone who plays the game can see your profile name and your scores on them, and, if they open your profile card, four stats: levels completed, stars, best daily streak and cup league. Nothing else about you is shown. Scores and stats are sent to the profile service when you play; the weekly and daily boards reset every week and every day at 12:30 pm IST, and no archive of past boards is kept.

Who processes it. Profiles and leaderboards run on Unity Gaming Services (Unity Authentication, Unity Leaderboards and Unity Cloud Code) provided by Unity Technologies, which acts for the developer. Unity assigns your profile a random player ID and processes the IP address your device connects from (to deliver the service and keep it secure). This is a separate Unity project from the one used for ads. Data is encrypted in transit. The developer can see and delete profiles and scores through Unity's tools. Unity handles this data under the Unity Privacy Policy.

How long it is kept. Your profile, name, scores and stats are kept until you delete your profile (see Your choices and deletion). Anonymous profiles cannot be recovered after you reinstall the game or lose your phone: a new profile with a new name is created instead, and the old one stays on the boards until it is deleted.

Ages 13 to 17. Players aged 13 to 17 get the same profile and appear on the boards in the same way as everyone else. The name is random and the game collects no real name, but if you are under 18 please ask a parent or guardian first, and email the address below if you want your profile removed.

The game keeps your progress, hints and settings on your device as before; the profile holds only the name, scores and stats described above.

Ads

The game shows two kinds of ad: a short full-screen ad when you leave a level you have finished, and rewarded ads that you choose to watch to earn a hint or an extra heart. There are no banner ads. Ads are provided by Unity LevelPlay (ironSource) and its ad network partner Unity Ads. The ad software starts in the background a few seconds after the game opens, whether or not you ever watch an ad, so that ads are ready when they are needed.

The game requests non-personalised ads. The game tells the ad services not to personalise ads, to treat you as not consenting to personalised advertising, not to sell your data, and that the game is not directed at children under 13. The game asks for the advertising-ID permission, so the ad services can read your Android advertising ID. They use it to deliver and measure ads, limit how often you see an ad and prevent fraud, and the game asks the ad services not to use it for personalised ads or interest profiles. The game does not ask for Android's ad-topics and ad-attribution permissions. You can reset or delete the advertising ID, or opt out of ads personalisation, in your device's ad settings (on most phones: Settings > Google > Ads, or Settings > Privacy > Ads).

From the moment the ad software starts, the ad services may receive technical data to deliver and measure ads and to prevent fraud, such as your approximate location (from your IP address), device and app identifiers including the advertising ID, device and operating-system information, diagnostics, and how you interact with the ad. Ad providers act under their own privacy policies: Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy. The developer does not receive this data and cannot identify you from it.

Analytics and crash reports

The game uses Google Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics.

  • Analytics: the game records a few anonymous gameplay events: a level or daily puzzle won, a level or daily puzzle failed, a hint used, and a hint pack bought. They carry no name or email, and the game does not attach your profile name to them. In Firebase Analytics (this does not apply to the ad services above) advertising features are turned off: advertising-ID collection, Google signals, ad personalisation, ad storage and ad user data are all disabled. Google Analytics keeps this event data for 2 months, then deletes it.
  • Crash reports: if the game crashes, Crashlytics sends a crash report: the technical error, the state of the app at the time, and basic device information (for example the device model and Android version) with a random installation identifier. It contains no personal data that the game adds. Firebase keeps crash data for 90 days before deleting it.

Firebase acts for the developer and processes this data under the Firebase privacy information and the Google Privacy Policy. Data is encrypted in transit.

Purchases (optional)

The game offers optional hint packs. Payments are handled entirely by Google Play; the developer never sees your card or payment details. The game learns only that a purchase of a given pack completed. It stores the Google Play order ID and your hint balance with your game progress so that each purchase is granted exactly once. The developer keeps no purchase records on its own servers. Google handles payments, refunds and receipts under the Google Play Terms of Service.

Your game progress

Your game progress, hint balance and order IDs are saved on your own device so you can carry on where you left off. The developer cannot see them, apart from the scores and stats that appear on the public leaderboards through your player profile (see above). Uninstalling the game or clearing its storage deletes the copy on your device. If you are signed in to Google Play Games, a copy is also kept in Play Games Saved Games as described above. If you have Android backup turned on, Android may include this save in your device backup to your Google account; that backup is handled by Google, not by the developer, and you control it in your device settings. Hints you bought are kept with this save, so deleting the save (and any Saved Games copy) also removes unused hints.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, the game schedules a few reminders on your device after you stop playing (a few hours apart, never at night) to let you know your next puzzle is waiting. Reminders are created and shown entirely on the device; no notification data is sent anywhere. You can turn them off in the game's settings or in your device's settings at any time.

Third parties

The game includes these third-party components: Google Play Games Services, Google Play Billing (through Unity In-App Purchasing), Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics, Unity LevelPlay with Unity Ads, and Unity Gaming Services (Authentication, Leaderboards and Cloud Code). The game requests non-personalised ads, and includes no data brokers and no social-media trackers. When you download the game, Google Play handles the download under Google's Privacy Policy, which the developer does not control. The developer does not sell your personal data.

Your choices and deletion

You can use the game without signing in, without watching ads and without buying anything. Notifications can be switched off at any time. The anonymous player profile is created automatically; you can delete it at any time as described below.

How to delete your Arrow Grid data:

  • Player profile and leaderboard scores (the anonymous profile, its name, scores and stats, stored on Unity Gaming Services). Two ways to delete them:
    1. In the game: open Me and tap "Delete my profile", then confirm. This removes your profile and all its scores and stats from the leaderboards. Your progress on your device stays. The game does not create a new profile until you choose to join the boards again.
    2. By email, if you cannot open the game, or lost your phone (an anonymous profile cannot be recovered after a reinstall): email dir.atishagrawal@gmail.com with your profile name including the number (for example "SwiftOtter#12345"; it is shown on your Me page and next to your scores on the boards). The developer deletes the profile and its scores within 30 days.
  • Play Games data (achievements, leaderboard scores and the progress saved with Play Games Saved Games) is stored by Google Play Games, not by the developer. To delete it in the Google Play Store app, tap your profile icon at the top right, then tap Settings > General > Play Games Profile > Privacy & Settings. Under "Your Data", tap Delete profile & game data; under "Delete individual game data", find Arrow Grid and tap Delete. On the web, go to play.google.com/games/profile, click Your Data > Delete Play Games Account & Data; under "Delete individual game data", find Arrow Grid and click Delete. The same page also lets you delete your whole Play Games profile and all of your Play Games data. Saved Games keeps its copy in a hidden area of your Google Drive; to remove only that copy, open Google Drive settings on the web, choose Manage apps, find Arrow Grid, then Options > Delete hidden app data. If you later sign in to Play Games again, the game re-sends the achievements and progress recorded on your device.
  • Analytics and crash reports contain no name or user ID, so the developer cannot look up one person's records. Analytics events are deleted automatically after 2 months and crash data after 90 days. If you want your device's analytics or crash data removed sooner, email the address below and the developer will ask Firebase to delete it as far as Firebase allows.
  • Ad data is held by the ad providers under their own privacy policies; you can send them a deletion request through their privacy pages. You can reset or delete your Android advertising ID in your device's ad settings (on most phones: Settings > Google > Ads, or Settings > Privacy > Ads).
  • Purchase records are held by Google Play; you can see them in your Google Account.
  • On your device: uninstall the game or clear its data.

Once your player profile is deleted, the developer keeps no copy of it or its scores. Apart from that profile, the developer keeps no copy of your gameplay data; Google's and the ad providers' own retention follows their policies. Visibility of Play Games achievements follows your Play Games profile visibility setting (friends only, everyone, or only you). Visibility of the game's own leaderboards does not depend on that setting: every player profile is public on them until deleted.

Children and teens

The game is not directed at children under 13 and is intended for players aged 13 and over. For every player, whatever their age, the game requests non-personalised ads, Firebase Analytics advertising features are off, and the game builds no advertising profile of you. Every player, aged 13 and over, also gets the same anonymous leaderboard profile (see Player profiles and leaderboards above). If you are under 18, please ask a parent or guardian before buying anything, watching ads or signing in to Google Play Games. A parent or guardian who believes the game holds data about their child can email the address below and the developer will act on the request. The developer does not knowingly collect personal data such as names, emails or phone numbers from anyone.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the game changes how it uses data, this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the effective date above will change.
